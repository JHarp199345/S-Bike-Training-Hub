Projected loads
Running load over time
Orange: a separate five-day exertion response. Blue: accumulated mechanical blocks. One provisional block uses the median impact of the first three recorded runs, with an estimated unconditioned baseline as a lower bound; it is a comparison unit, not a known safe dose. Each run adds load based on estimated steps and ground force, with an extra cost when load is already present. The blue curve stays high for about five days per block, then declines over about three days per block, to a fifth of its level, followed by a remodeling tail of about four months. There's no cap: 13 blocks holds about 65 days and declines over about 39. Gray marks show recorded run days; dotted lines assume no new runs. The 1.5-block line is a provisional running limit. Feet, leg, and hop reports during the plateau affect the run/rest decision without moving this accumulated curve. After the plateau, those reports can adjust the decline duration by at most half a day each. Knee, ankle, and Achilles forces are related internal loads, not additional forces summed into the score. These are planning assumptions, not measured tissue damage or a diagnosis.
